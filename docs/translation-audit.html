The short answer

No — not reliably enough to put in front of a congregation.

Roughly two thirds of the segments are accurate, and the fluent ones are genuinely good: idiomatic, correctly inflected, right register. That is also the problem. Nothing on screen signals which third is wrong, and the failures are not garbled text a reader would discount — they are clean, confident sentences that happen to assert the reverse of what was said.

The worst of them: the sermon’s line that the steward’s unethical behaviour is never endorsed reaches all three languages as “it was endorsed”. “Jesus praises shrewdness, not dishonesty” becomes “Jesus praises cleverness, not honesty” in Malayalam and Telugu. “Good intentions without action accomplish nothing” becomes an instruction to the congregation: “do not accomplish anything.”

Most of this is not the translation model failing at translation. It is the segmenter handing the model half a sentence — and a verb-final language cannot defer meaning the way English can, so the model guesses, and commits.

Root cause

A four-second hold, waiting on speech that arrives in eight.

pipeline.py is built on exactly the right insight, stated in its own comment: “ml/te/hi are verb-final, so half an English sentence forces the model to invent a verb and then strand the remainder.” So it holds any utterance that does not end in .?!… and joins it to the next one. The guard is correct. The timeout is set below the thing it waits for.

Why a fragment is worse in these three languages than in English

English defers its verb; Malayalam, Telugu and Hindi put it last. When the model is handed “The man’s ability to un-” it cannot leave a hole where the predicate goes — the grammar demands one, so it supplies one. That is why three of the five reversals in this audit are the same mechanism: a negation (never), a subject (good intentions) or a head noun (ability) sat in the previous segment, and the sentence left behind was grammatical, confident and wrong.

Two smaller register slips worth a look

Segment 28 renders God as Hindi भगवान, the generic term, where the BSI Hindi edition named in config.py uses परमेश्वर. All four render inconsistently across phones.

What to change

Ordered by how much of the damage each one removes.

The first two are small, contained edits and together they account for most of what is on this page.

01

Raise the sentence-hold timeout above the chunk cadence

pipeline.py · MAX_SENTENCE_HOLD_S in config.py

At 4.0 s the hold expires before its continuation arrives 93% of the time, so the guard that exists specifically to prevent fragment translation almost never fires. The observed median gap is 7.9 s and MAX_BUFFER_S is 8.0. Set the hold to 10–12 s so it spans at least one full audio chunk. This alone addresses segments 18, 19, 22, 23 and 24 — three of the five reversals.

English captions already publish immediately and independently, so raising this delays only the translated line, and only for sentences that are genuinely still in progress.

02

Add repetition control to the decode call

translator.py · IndicTrans2Translator.translate_all_sync

generate() currently passes max_length and num_beams and nothing else. Segment 3 ran to the full 256-token cap emitting a single repeated character in all three languages, taking 4.34 s against a 1.10 s mean. Add no_repeat_ngram_size=4 and repetition_penalty=1.15.

This also caps the worst-case latency, which matters because the GPU lock serialises the queue behind whichever segment is slowest.

03

Strip trailing ellipses before the text reaches the translator

pipeline.py · _flush

The runaway in segment 3 was seeded by a trailing .... A dangling ellipsis carries no meaning into the target and reliably invites the loop — drop it, along with trailing commas, on the way into translate_all.

04

Give the translator the previous sentence as context

translator.py

Terminology drift, the honorific inversion and the wrong-antecedent errors all come from the same place: each segment is translated with no memory of the one before it. Prepending the previous flushed sentence and discarding its translation costs one longer forward pass and gives the model something to be consistent with.

If that proves too slow for three targets, the cheaper approximation is a pinned glossary for the terms that actually recur — steward, master, commission, sons of light — applied as a post-pass.

05

Tighten the silence gates, or gate on the English side

stt.py · keep_segment · config.py

Eight phantom segments in 74 seconds. Rather than extending the blocklist, which config.py rightly resists, consider dropping any segment under ~3 words that arrives with no speech in the preceding window — that catches “Ugh.”, “Mm.” and “Yeah.” without touching a real “Amen.”

06

Normalise the Malayalam output encoding

a post-pass on the ml string

Map legacy ൻറ and ൻ്റ to standard ന്റ, and the deprecated ൌ to ൗ, before the text reaches the screens.
